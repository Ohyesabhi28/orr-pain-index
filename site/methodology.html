How the index works.

How the Pain Index is measured, and what it cannot tell you.

The Pain Index

For each route we compare the current travel time with the free-flow time, which is the time with no traffic.

index = clamp((live / free-flow − 1) / (3 − 1), 0, 1) × 100

The result is rounded to a whole number. The current ceiling is 3× free flow: a trip that takes three times as long as free flow scores 100, and a trip at free flow scores 0. Minutes lost is live time minus free-flow time, never below zero.

Worked example. A trip that takes 55 minutes when free flow is 20 minutes has a ratio of 2.75. That gives (2.75 − 1) / 2 = 0.875, so the index is 88, which is Gridlock, and 35 minutes are lost.

Data source and schedule

Travel times come from the TomTom Routing API: one request per route, giving the live and the no-traffic travel time. We poll every 15 minutes between 05:37 and 23:22 IST.

Medians and minimum samples

The heatmap shows the median index for each weekday and 30-minute slot. A cell is shown only with at least 3 observations from 3 different weeks. Until then it is hatched and marked “still collecting”. Best and worst departure windows are picked from the cells that qualify, separately for the morning (7–11 am) and evening (4:30–9 pm) peaks. Trip times on those cards are estimates, worked back from the index and the route’s free-flow time.

Sanity bounds

A reading is discarded, and counted as missing, if any time or distance is zero or negative, or if the live time is less than half the free-flow time. Failed requests are retried once, then recorded as missing.

Limitations

  • Only the fixed set of routes is measured, not the whole road network.
  • Data is collected at 15-minute resolution, so short spikes between polls are missed.
  • The free-flow time is modelled by TomTom, not measured on an empty road.
  • Skipped or failed runs leave gaps; the dashboard does not fill them in.
  • Not navigation advice. Historical patterns are not a promise.
